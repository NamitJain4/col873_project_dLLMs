# Controlled membership and canary experiment

Generate `data/processed/synthetic_mia/` with `python3 scripts/prepare_synthetic_mia.py`. The generator creates fictional email-shaped text with one verification phrase per candidate. For each repetition group (1, 4, 16), it creates 32 members and 32 nonmembers from the same template and randomization process. Only members are included in training; the corresponding training record is repeated the assigned number of times. A separate background set of 512 routine fictional emails keeps the task from consisting entirely of canaries.

The resulting `candidates.jsonl` is the ground-truth MIA set, `train.jsonl` is the fine-tuning corpus, and `nonmembers.jsonl` is an explicit holdout. The entire dataset is ignored by Git. The files contain no real addresses or personal data. The split generator asserts that canary phrases, prompts, and texts are unique among candidates, and that member and nonmember texts do not overlap.

Before fine-tuning, run `../col873_ass2/.venv-gpu/bin/python scripts/audit_mia_split.py` from the repository root. This checks unique texts and canaries, balanced repetition strata, character lengths, and a five-fold text-only classifier. Its AUROC should be near chance; otherwise revise the split. Keep the candidate list and random seed fixed across models. Evaluate fine-tuning membership rather than attempting to infer undocumented pretraining membership.

On the initial 192-candidate split, the blind text-only classifier scored AUROC 0.4749 (five folds, seed 123). Treat this as an initial distribution-shift check. For the final analysis, rerun it over several split seeds and report uncertainty.

For each model, compare baseline, exact deduplication, and Goldfish-style loss omission under matched optimizer steps and token budgets. The omission mask should be stable for a candidate across repeated copies; independent random omission per copy would eventually supervise most positions. For dLLMs, keep this omission mask separate from the sampled denoising mask. Measure exact canary recovery from the stored prefix, model-specific loss-based MIA AUROC, chosen-mask reconstruction success, held-out task utility, and confidence intervals across seeds. Repetition-group slices are required: an overall AUROC can hide a strong high-copy effect.

The corpus preparation and text-only split audit do not establish any privacy result. The short training checks below also cannot estimate full-data leakage.

On 2026-11-17, short LoRA engineering checks were queued for Qwen and LLaDA. Both use the same fictional completion instruction, 16 training rows, rank 8 adapters on query/value projections, response-only loss, learning rate `1e-4`, accumulation 8, and seed 42. The AR loss is causal cross-entropy on assistant tokens. The diffusion loss masks only assistant tokens, keeps the prompt visible, and weights masked-token cross-entropy by inverse mask probability. A dependent full pipeline trains on all 1,184 rows for each of the four assigned models only if both short training and attack checks succeed. The paired extraction evaluator uses exact canary recovery plus response log-likelihood (AR) or eight repeated 30% response-mask scores (diffusion); the latter is a model-specific attack score, not a comparable likelihood across architectures. The full pipeline validates all 192 candidates per base/tuned model before aggregation.

A second dependent pipeline trains a Goldfish-style variant with a 25% stable random loss-omission mask over assistant response positions. The mask is keyed by record id and seed, so all copies of one canary omit the same target positions. It is separate from the diffusion denoising mask. A third dependent job scores 128 new fictional routine emails against base, full-trained, and Goldfish variants. These are matched one-seed comparisons. Any utility claim uses paired within-model response scores; raw AR and diffusion score magnitudes are not comparable. Exact extraction and MIA AUROC need uncertainty intervals and repetition-group slices before interpretation.
